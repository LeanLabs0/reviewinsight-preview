Methodology

What we read, how the score is worked out, and what we deliberately do not do. Every figure on the site can be rebuilt from the numbers on the vendor pages.

Where the data comes from

For every vendor we read four platforms and record the average rating, the number of reviews, and a sample of review text with its date. Each figure carries the page it came from and the day we checked it. Nothing is typed in by hand.

PlatformVendors coveredWhat we take
G216 of 16Rating, review count, review text with dates
Capterra16 of 16Rating, review count, review text with dates
Gartner Peer Insights15 of 16Rating, review and rating counts, review headlines with dates. Bodies are login-gated.
Trustpilot15 of 16Rating, review count, review text with dates

The score

Two things decide it. What reviewers rate a product, and how much that rating can be trusted. We publish both, and the second one adjusts the first.

rating = the four sites' own averages, averaged, x20
evidence = 0.30 Recent + 0.25 Reliable + 0.25 Results + 0.20 Resonance
score = 50 + (rating - 50) x evidence / 100

A product with perfect evidence scores exactly what reviewers rate it. A product with no usable evidence sits at 50, because we do not know anything about it. Everything else lands in between, moved away from the middle only as far as its evidence earns.

score = (Recent + Reliable + Results‑specific + Resonance) / 4
  • RecentHow strong the review evidence is in the last 180 days.
  • ReliableWhether the evidence holds up: do the written reviews match their star ratings, does the rating pattern look natural, do reviews arrive steadily or in bursts.
  • Results‑specificHow often reviewers name a concrete outcome you could check, rather than saying it saves time.
  • ResonanceWhether reviewers recommend the product or merely tolerate it.

A vendor with fewer than two sites carrying reviews, under 15 reviews, or under 5 reviews in the last year will be shown as Not Rated rather than given a number.

Where each number comes from

PartMeasured from
RecentHow many days since the newest review, and what share of the reviews we hold were written in the last year.
ReliableHow far apart the sites' star ratings are, and how often a review's wording matches the stars it was given.
Results-specificThe share of reviews naming an outcome a stranger could check. A number, a time saved, a tool replaced. Not "saves time".
ResonanceWhether reviewers recommend the product, approve of it, tolerate it, or warn you off. Averaged per site, then across sites.

Two of the four need the review text read. A model labels each review and has to quote the exact words behind its label; if those words are not literally in the review, the label is thrown away. Every calculation is done in code, never by the model.

G2 gives us no working pagination, so we read it through four sort orders. Two of those return the best and worst reviews on purpose, so we use them for quotes and keep them out of every rate. Gartner keeps review text behind a login, so it contributes ratings and dates but not tone.

What we will not do

  • No paid placementA vendor cannot buy a rank, a badge, or a better score. There is no vendor tier to sell.
  • No invented numbersIf we cannot read a figure we show that we could not, with the counts we do have. We never fill a gap with an estimate.
  • No republished reviewsQuotes are trimmed to 40 words, attributed, dated, and linked to the original. Reviewer names are dropped when we read the page, so we never store them.
  • No hidden arithmeticEvery score will show its inputs and its sum on the page, so you can redo it.

Known gaps

Gartner Peer Insights puts most review bodies behind a login. We use its headline, rating and date, and do not touch the gated text. Two vendors in this preview have no listing on one platform, which we show rather than hide.
